I am designing a modular, large-scale engineering team-building activity: a collaborative marble-run challenge for approximately 10 teams.

PROJECT CONCEPT
Participants are senior engineering professionals. Each team receives a modular challenge section and must design/assemble a marble pathway that connects to other teams' sections. The final system should transport a steel ball from a common START to a common FINISH through all modules.

VENUE
Expected usable area is approximately 3,000 sq ft, potentially a large hall/courtyard. The venue may be roughly 50 ft × 50 ft or similar, but exact dimensions are not yet confirmed.

MARBLE
- Steel bearing balls
- Approximately 25 mm diameter
- Bearing-grade steel preferred
- The marble must roll freely through the pathway.

PATHWAY
Primary pathway material:
- 1½-inch Schedule 40 PVC pipe
- Also potentially 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC
- Pipes will be pre-cut into approximately 1 m sections for transport and participant assembly.
- Clear flexible pneumatic tubing may also be available for visually interesting sections.
- Participants should be able to connect multiple pipe sections without permanent glue.
- Couplers, unions, T-junctions, Y-junctions, 3-way junctions etc. may be used.
- Connections must preserve smooth ball flow and avoid snagging.

STRUCTURE
I initially considered 4040 and 3030 8-mm T-slot aluminium extrusion, pre-cut into 1 m sections, but the cost is proving very high.
I am therefore investigating cheaper structural alternatives such as:
- aluminium angle/channel
- lightweight PVC electrical conduit
- other lightweight modular framing systems.

The structure must be:
- lightweight
- safe for participant handling
- stable
- reusable
- modular
- quick to assemble
- capable of supporting elevated PVC marble pathways.

3D PRINTING
I can 3D-print custom:
- PVC saddle clamps
- pipe holders
- sensor sleeves
- structural connectors
- adapters
- specialized joints

Likely material:
- PETG
- black PETG for sensor housings where useful.

ACTIVITY DESIGN
There will be approximately 10 teams/modules.

Each module should have:
- a defined marble IN point
- a defined marble OUT point
- an engineering challenge
- enough freedom for participants to design their own solution
- standardized interfaces so modules can connect.

The challenge should be genuinely interesting to experienced engineers without requiring precision machining, gears, ratchets or complex mechanisms.

Available construction materials should primarily be:
- PVC pipe
- clear flexible pneumatic tube
- lightweight structural members
- 3D-printed components
- simple fasteners.

ELECTRONICS
I am designing approximately 20 independent ESP32 sensor nodes.

Each module may have two measurement points, with each measurement point consisting of:
- IR LED transmitter
- IR phototransistor receiver

The two optical beams are placed a known fixed distance apart. When the marble breaks each beam, the ESP32 records timestamps.

This allows:
velocity = known distance / time between beam interruptions.

Each sensor node is intended to be:
- self-contained
- battery powered
- enclosed in a small 3D-printed PETG housing
- connected to the venue Wi-Fi
- removable from the physical marble-run.

NETWORK
A TP-Link Archer AX53-class Wi-Fi router will create a local, offline Wi-Fi network.

Approximately 20 ESP32 nodes connect over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

A laptop connects to the same local network.

No Internet connection is required.

The laptop runs the central dashboard/GUI.

The system should continue to work entirely locally even if the WAN/Internet connection is disconnected.

POWER
Each ESP32 node uses a small 1S LiPo battery, approximately:
- 3.7 V nominal
- 500 mAh

Each node should include:
- LiPo charging
- power switch
- battery voltage measurement
- battery status indication.

RGB LED
Each sensor node will have a 5 mm, 4-pin RGB LED, preferably common cathode, although common anode is also acceptable.

Battery status could be:
- Green = healthy
- Yellow = getting low
- Red = low
- flashing red = critically low

The ESP32 should measure LiPo voltage using an ADC and voltage divider.

SENSING
The optical sensor should be a simple IR break-beam system:
IR LED → air gap → phototransistor.

The 25 mm steel ball interrupts the beam.

Avoid TV-remote-style 38-kHz IR receiver modules; use a bare IR emitter + phototransistor arrangement.

GUI
The central laptop dashboard should display:
- modules/nodes
- ball detected
- ball passed
- timestamps
- measured velocity where appropriate
- node battery status
- connectivity/heartbeat status
- potentially overall elapsed time.

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
This is an engineering leadership/team-building event rather than a commercial production machine.

Prioritize:
1. Safety
2. Reliability
3. Ease of participant assembly
4. Fast setup/reset
5. Reusability
6. Low cost
7. Clear measurement
8. Interesting engineering constraints.
