# Ball and Bend — what decides whether the run works

## Clearance

| Ball | In 28mm ID | Occupancy |
|---|---|---|
| 12 mm | 8 mm gap | 43% |
| 25 mm | 1.5 mm gap | 89% |

Below about half the bore a ball ricochets rather than rolls — it wanders off the centreline and scrubs speed unpredictably. Above about two-thirds it's channelled and repeatable.

## Bend radius — the constraint that matters

A ball is rigid, a bend is curved. The ball can't hug the curve, so it cuts across and presses into the outer wall. The tighter the bend, the harder it presses.

**Minimum centreline bend radius ≈ r² / 2(a − r)** where r = ball radius, a = tube inner radius.

For a 25mm ball in 28mm ID: **R ≥ 52mm**.

| | Pushes ball outward | 1.5mm available | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32mm short elbow (R ≈ 40mm) | ~2mm | not enough | **jams** |
| 32mm long-radius bend (R ≈ 95mm) | ~1mm | enough | passes |

The 12mm ball needs only R ≥ 5mm, so it passes everything including sharp corners.

**Consequence:** short 90° elbows are impassable for the 25mm ball and perfectly usable for the 12mm one. Path B teams holding short elbows physically cannot use them; Path A teams can. That's a real trade emerging from the physics, discoverable in about ninety seconds of testing.

## The other constriction — cut ends

A PVC scissor cutter works by pinching, and deforms the bore inward by 1–2mm at the cut. With 1.5mm clearance that alone can stop a 25mm ball. **Reaming after every cut is mandatory, not good practice.** It's also the strongest argument for the cutting bay producing square, undeformed cuts.

## Not a constriction — socket shoulders

At a solvent-weld joint the pipe seats into a socket bored to the pipe's **OD**, and the shoulder where that socket steps down to the waterway faces away from a ball travelling inside the pipe. Pipe ID ≈ 28mm, fitting waterway ≈ 28mm. The ball goes 28 → 28.

## Launcher energy

Team 2 must climb 1.5 ft **above** launch height, so the ball needs ≥ 3.0 m/s at the foot of the climb — call it 4–5 m/s at launch after losses. That needs a real elastic launcher, not a gravity ramp.

With a shared trigger, a lighter ball takes far more velocity from the same stored energy — roughly 3× for a 12mm against a 25mm — and climb height goes as v². That's why the small ball takes the climbing path.
